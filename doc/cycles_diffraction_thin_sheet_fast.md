# Thin Wall Fast completed phase screen

The nonzero-relief model retains native Thin Wall at exact zero diffraction weight or depth. For constant passive inputs it adds a cached reciprocal microscopic sheet component and blends continuously from the original native sheet. It is a scalar phase-screen approximation of an exterior-air sheet, not a Maxwell solution of a corrugated pair of boundaries.

For each facet/order, the projected first-event kernel uses the smaller of the forward and reverse endpoint sheet-coefficient × order-power products. The GGX geometry is unchanged. The zero-order residual is evaluated at both endpoints because the set of propagating diffraction orders can differ. This microscopic envelope is reciprocal and cannot increase the uncompensated first-event escape budget.

The scalar spectral sheet coefficients sum front/back lossless-film Fresnel and Beer absorption using the native Thin Wall geometric-series convention. Reflection tint remains an RGB coefficient; chromatic transmission tint follows the actual Cycles BT709 spectral reconstruction at each cache wavelength. Transmission roughness is derived from the wavelength-dependent IOR. Travel and reciprocal measure are in air on both sheet sides.

Let `S(w)` be the sheet reflection-plus-transmission budget and `E(w)` the escaped microscopic-envelope energy. The cache stores `q(w)=S(w)-E(w)`. Its return kernel is `q(i) q(o)/Q`, where `Q` integrates the same finite angular interpolant over both air hemispheres. Existing albedo values, descriptors and projected averages are reused; `Q=2*pi*average(q)`. Fourfold azimuth symmetrization preserves row sums and gives the finite cache the sheet's reflection symmetries. Runtime lookup folds the two tangent components to their absolute values, preserving transmission reversal even with a finite VNDF estimator.

The compatibility blend is the fixed material-only convention `b=clamp(32*max(1-P0_reflect,1-P0_transmit),0,1)`. The completed component reaches full strength at 1/32 redistributed phase power and vanishes continuously toward zero relief. This threshold is not fitted to an energy render. The completed component is reciprocal; the native contribution retains the original incoming-only compensation and is not claimed strictly reciprocal. Native weight is `1-diffraction_weight*b`, and completed-component weight is `diffraction_weight*b`. The original native endpoint retains its unmodified d-line IOR and transmission roughness, even if the completed component is dispersed. Reusing a dispersed carrier at that endpoint would create a tiny-depth discontinuity. A pure reciprocal nonzero model would also create an unacceptable discontinuity from the native rough sheet.

First-event and cosine-return lobes are separate closures, including smooth atoms. This keeps BDPT delta classification, sample/evaluation PDFs and guiding metadata consistent. A shared extra occupies two 96-byte closure slots. The full completed sheet uses six slots; partial coverage or a compatibility transition additionally retains two native slots. Insufficient capacity creates no partial completed material or silent native substitution. The actual all-layer Principled graph fits the reserved 24 slots (23 used for partial coverage).

The shipping cache uses 24 squared-mu nodes, 16 azimuth bins, at least 16 wavelength nodes and 512 VNDF facets. Coated wavelengths increase with the film optical period. A bounded work limit applies. Constant passive inputs with wavelength-dependent IOR at least one use the completed cache; linked inputs, nonpassive inputs, caustic-disabled ports or requests beyond the bounded cache model retain the existing explicitly approximate route. Exact zero still uses the original native setup.
